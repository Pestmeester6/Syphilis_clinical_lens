About the series

Syphilis continues to pose an important clinical and public health challenge across the Region of the Americas, including the resurgence of adult and congenital syphilis. This webinar series brings clinical updates together with programmatic, laboratory, surveillance, and implementation perspectives.

Developed jointly by the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) and the WHO Collaborating Centre for Sexually Transmitted Infections at the London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine (LSHTM), the series brings together regional and international experts in STI clinical practice, diagnostics, research, and public health.

Each session uses a case-based format designed for practical application in clinical and public-health settings. Sessions are intended for health professionals, public health practitioners, laboratory professionals, programme managers, and others working in syphilis prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and elimination.

Diagnosis and laboratory approaches

ChairJoseph D. TuckerUniversity of North Carolina at Chapel Hill · LSHTM
Evidence presentationValerie WilsonCaribbean Med Labs Foundation
Case presenterTina Hylton-KongUniversity of the West Indies, Mona

Key topics

Clinical staging and natural history; treponemal and non-treponemal testing algorithms; rapid and dual HIV/syphilis tests; interpretation of titres; diagnosis of neurosyphilis.

Learning objectives

  • Recognise the clinical features of primary, secondary, latent, and tertiary syphilis
  • Select and interpret appropriate serological tests, including reverse-sequence algorithms
  • Identify when cerebrospinal fluid examination and neurosyphilis evaluation are indicated

Publications and resources

Sweitzer S, Duncan JA, Seña AC. Update on syphilis diagnostics. Curr Opin Infect Dis. 2025 Feb 1;38(1):44-53. doi: 10.1097/QCO.0000000000001073. Epub 2024 Dec 6. PMID: 39641765; PMCID: PMC11695141.

Papp JR, Park IU, Fakile Y, Pereira L, Pillay A, Bolan GA. CDC Laboratory Recommendations for Syphilis Testing, United States, 2024. MMWR Recomm Rep 2024;73(No. RR-1):1–32. DOI: http://dx.doi.org/10.15585/mmwr.rr7301a1.

Session summary

The webinar focused on the diagnosis of syphilis, with Dr. Tina Hylton-Kong and Ms. Valerie Wilson as the main speakers. Dr. Hylton-Kong discussed the epidemiology, natural history, and clinical staging of syphilis, including primary, secondary, latent, and tertiary stages, and presented two clinical cases to illustrate diagnosis and management. She emphasized the importance of thorough history-taking, physical examination, and appropriate serological testing, including treponemal and non-treponemal tests, and highlighted the need for follow-up and evaluation for neurosyphilis in certain cases. Ms. Wilson provided an in-depth overview of serological testing algorithms, comparing traditional and reverse testing sequences, and discussed the role of rapid diagnostic tests, dual HIV-syphilis tests, and nucleic acid amplification techniques. She explained the limitations and interpretation challenges of different tests and the importance of quality assurance in laboratory practices. The session concluded with a Q&A where questions about complex cases, diagnostic challenges, and regional issues were addressed.

Treatment and clinical management

ChairMichael MarksLSHTM · University College London Hospitals
Evidence presentationAdele Schwartz BenzakenAIDS Healthcare Foundation
Case presenterDee Miller

Key topics

Benzathine penicillin G regimens by stage; management of penicillin allergy; alternatives and their evidence base; treatment of neurosyphilis; follow-up and definitions of treatment failure versus reinfection.

Learning objectives

  • Prescribe stage-appropriate treatment in line with current PAHO/WHO guidance
  • Manage penicillin allergy, treatment reactions, and complex presentations
  • Distinguish treatment failure from reinfection and act on serological follow-up

Publications and resources

Ziaya PR, Hankins GDV, Gilstrap LC, Halsey AB. Intravenous Penicillin Desensitization and Treatment During Pregnancy. JAMA. 1986;256(18):2561–2562. doi:10.1001/jama.1986.03380180123032

Session summary

This session explored challenges in diagnosing, treating, and monitoring syphilis through clinical cases involving ocular disease and infection during pregnancy. Discussions emphasized selecting treatment according to disease stage and clinical manifestations, with penicillin remaining the preferred therapy. The presenters distinguished the Jarisch–Herxheimer reaction from true penicillin allergy and highlighted the importance of appropriate allergy assessment before excluding penicillin. Alternative treatments were reviewed, alongside their evidence limitations and the role of desensitization during pregnancy when indicated. The session also addressed sexual partner management to interrupt transmission and prevent reinfection. Clinical and serological follow-up featured prominently, including interpretation of changes in nontreponemal test titres to assess treatment response and investigate possible reinfection or treatment failure. Throughout the discussion, speakers stressed that interpretation requires consideration of baseline titres, disease stage, and subsequent sexual exposures, and that management should follow national protocols while ensuring patients remain engaged in care throughout treatment and subsequent follow-up.

Linkage to care

ChairEmma SlaymakerLondon School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine
Evidence presentationAngelica Espinosa MirandaFederal University of Espírito Santo (UFES)
Case presenterMayara Secco Torres SilvaINI–Fiocruz, Rio de Janeiro

Key topics

Define linkage to syphilis care; existing systems for linkage to care; barriers to linkage to care; interventions to increase linkage to care.

Learning objectives

  • Identify main challenges with linkage to care in line with current PAHO/WHO guidance
  • Describe key implementation strategies to promote linkage to care
  • Describe health system approaches to linkage to care

Publications and resources

Tang EC, Segura ER, Clark JL, et al The syphilis care cascade: tracking the course of care after screening positive among men and transgender women who have sex with men in Lima, Peru BMJ Open 2015;5:e008552. doi: 10.1136/bmjopen-2015-008552

Session summary

This session examined how social circumstances and health system barriers affect syphilis diagnosis, treatment completion, and prevention of congenital infection. Two clinical cases, involving a young woman experiencing socioeconomic vulnerability and a man living with HIV, illustrated missed opportunities for diagnosis, delayed review of results, and difficulties completing treatment. Work commitments, financial constraints, concerns about adverse effects, scheduling difficulties, stigma, and confidentiality emerged as barriers. The discussion emphasized that linkage to care requires a coordinated pathway from testing through treatment completion, extending beyond referral alone. Speakers highlighted the need to translate WHO recommendations into practical workflows, supported by reliable penicillin supplies and professionals trained to administer treatment. Proposed approaches included flexible service hours, provider reminders, digital tools, patient education, and community engagement. The session reinforced person-centred care, integration with sexual and reproductive health services, and using every healthcare encounter as an opportunity to offer prevention, testing, treatment, and support for continuity.

Public health and key populations

ChairPhilippe MayaudLondon School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine
Evidence presentationAriel Eduardo Campos LozaUniversity of Guadalajara

Key topics

Epidemiology of resurgence among men who have sex with men, sex workers, transgender populations, and people living with HIV; integration of syphilis services into HIV, antenatal services, and primary care; partner services; surveillance; emerging prevention tools including doxycycline post-exposure prophylaxis (DoxyPEP).

Learning objectives

  • Apply integrated service delivery models that combine syphilis with HIV platforms
  • Understand the epidemiology of syphilis in key populations
  • Critically appraise the evidence on emerging prevention strategies such as DoxyPEP

Publications and resources

Chircop O, Jaggers C, Spiteri M, Schembri A, Padovese V. DOXY do, or DOXY Don't? Syphilis and doxycycline post-exposure prophylaxis: A case report. Int J STD AIDS. 2025 Mar;36(4):324-326. doi: 10.1177/09564624241308026. Epub 2024 Dec 17. PMID: 39689342.

Session summary

The session addressed syphilis management in key populations, using two clinical cases to illustrate differences in vulnerability and access to prevention. The discussion addressed factors contributing to a high syphilis burden, including higher HIV prevalence, interconnected sexual networks, and limited access to prevention and treatment. The cases involved Deborah, a transgender woman in prison who had recently acquired syphilis, and Pedro, a heterosexual man seeking sexual health advice regarding sex with transgender women. The session reviewed prevention strategies, including doxycycline post-exposure prophylaxis (doxy-PEP), and highlighted structural barriers that restrict access to preventive tools, particularly in prison settings. The session concluded with recommendations on testing frequency, partner notification, and tailoring prevention strategies to individual risk and the structural conditions affecting each population.

Syphilis in pregnancy and congenital syphilis

ChairEmma Harding-EschLondon School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine
Evidence presentationLindley A. BarbeeUS Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
Case presenterKate MieleUS Centers for Disease Control and Prevention

Key topics

Antenatal screening strategies; treatment in pregnancy and partner management; evaluation and treatment of the exposed and infected neonate; PAHO case definitions; the EMTCT Plus framework.

Learning objectives

  • Implement antenatal screening and treatment to prevent vertical syphilis transmission
  • Evaluate and manage the neonate exposed to or infected with syphilis
  • Apply EMTCT Plus indicators and case definitions in clinical and programmatic practice

Publications and resources

Andrade ALMB, Magalhães PVVS, Moraes MM, Tresoldi AT, Pereira RM. Late diagnosis of congenital syphilis: a recurring reality in women and children health care in Brazil. Rev Paul Pediatr. 2018;36(3):376–381. doi:10.1590/1984-0462/;2018;36;3;00011.

Syphilis Tools app — University of Washington

CDC STI Treatment Guidelines 2021 and mobile app

Session summary

This session focused on syphilis during pregnancy and congenital syphilis. Lindley Barbee presented global and regional epidemiological data, highlighting the continuing burden of preventable congenital infection. Kate Miele presented clinical cases illustrating diagnosis and treatment during pregnancy and management of neonates exposed to syphilis. The presenters reviewed screening recommendations, benzathine penicillin G treatment, and challenges related to testing and treatment shortages. They also introduced practical clinical resources, including the Syphilis Tools app and the STI Treatment Guide app, to support diagnosis and management. The session concluded with questions and answers addressing clinical scenarios and treatment decisions.
